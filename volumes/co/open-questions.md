# Open Questions: Colorado Volume

Interpretation questions the authoring pass could not settle from the text, each with the assumption the draft uses so the codex stays evaluable. Items: lists the items that carry the assumption.

### OQ-1. Pub. L. 119-21 sec. 10108 narrowed the non-citizen categories eligible for SNAP; Colorado's rule manual (10 CCR 2506-1 4.305) and 7 CFR 273.4 have not been updated to match

Body: The amended 7 U.S.C. 2015(f) (S8, S9) names only four eligible categories: citizens/nationals, LPRs, Cuban/Haitian entrants, and individuals lawfully residing under a Compact of Free Association. Refugees, asylees, parolees, and the other "qualified alien" categories of 8 U.S.C. 1641 are no longer eligible under the statute. 10 CCR 2506-1 4.305 (S10-S17) and 7 CFR 273.4 (S18) still describe the pre-H.R.1 scheme and list COFA migrants as not qualified. The draft follows the amended statute for the category list (refugees and asylees ineligible; COFA migrants eligible) and 8 U.S.C. 1612-1613 (S21-S24) for the five-year bar and its exceptions, which sec. 10108 did not amend and which Colorado restates at 4.305,B,3. Two points remain open: whether the five-year bar reaches Cuban/Haitian entrants (they are qualified aliens under 8 U.S.C. 1641(b)(7), not fetched, and 1613(b) exempts refugees and asylees but not entrants; the draft treats them as eligible without a wait, following the amended 2015(f) list), and the date from which FNS treats sec. 10108 as effective for current participants (not stated in D-5; the draft applies it as of the determination date). A future revision should replace this with the CDHS guidance implementing sec. 10108 once issued. Items: CO-306, CO-307, CO-308, CO-309, CO-310.

### OQ-2. Whether "elderly or disabled lawfully residing on August 22, 1996" (per the brief) is the same category as 10 CCR 2506-1 4.305,B,3,e (born on or before August 22, 1931)

Body: The brief describes an additional-condition exemption for "elderly/disabled who were lawfully residing on 8/22/1996." The only text found in the fetched Colorado or federal documents matching that date is 10 CCR 2506-1 4.305,B,3,e (S15), which is framed as a date-of-birth cutoff (born on or before August 22, 1931, i.e., age 65 or older as of that date) combined with lawful residence on that date, not a general elderly-or-disabled exemption. CO-306 (snap_is_exempt_from_immigrant_waiting_period; the fact snap_household_size (CO-313) is not the item with this logic - N-3 fix-round correction) models S15 literally (date_of_birth on or before 1931-08-22 and qualified_status_date on or before 1996-08-22, as a proxy for "lawfully resided ... on August 22, 1996" since no supplied fact captures residence on a specific historical date directly), rather than the broader "elderly or disabled" reading from the brief. Items: CO-306.

### OQ-3. No supplied fact establishes United States residency in itself; snap_meets_citizenship_requirement omits 7 U.S.C. 2015(f)(1)'s residency prong

Body: The amended 7 U.S.C. 2015(f)(1) (S8) requires "a resident of the United States" in addition to the status prong. No supplied fact in this volume's interface captures residency as distinct from `state_of_residence` (a text field naming the state). The draft treats a known `state_of_residence` as sufficient evidence of U.S. residency and does not add a further supplied fact; residency verification itself is out of this volume's scope per docs/design-colorado.md. Items: CO-307.

### OQ-11. Resolved (fix-round C-6): BCE's OAP/AND/AB prong now has its own supplied fact

Body: 10 CCR 2506-1 4.206,C,1,a (S47) lists SSI, Colorado Works (CW/TANF), Old Age Pension (OAP), Aid to the Needy Disabled (AND), and Aid to the Blind (AB) as the benefits that confer BCE when every household member receives or is authorized to receive one of them. This question originally noted that `snap_household_all_members_receive_categorical_assistance` (CO-336) read `receives_general_assistance` as a stand-in for OAP/AND/AB. On review (C-6) that substitution was found to be wrong, not merely approximate: `receives_general_assistance`'s own stated meaning is general assistance "based on disability or blindness criteria" (the 271.2 elderly-or-disabled prong CO-302 needs), and OAP is an age-based program (65 and over, or 60 with AND), so a household of pure OAP recipients -- the commonest BCE household in Colorado -- was not BCE under the model as written. Fixed by adding `receives_state_cash_assistance` (CO-073) for the OAP/AND/AB prong and having CO-336 read it instead; `receives_general_assistance` is left to CO-302 alone. Items: CO-336, CO-338, CO-340, CO-073.

### OQ-12. Whether the TANF/MOE non-cash-service notice condition of ECE (S48) is modeled as always met

Body: 10 CCR 2506-1 4.206,C,2,a,2) (S48) makes ECE conditional on the household having "been authorized to receive" a non-cash TANF/MOE-funded service, and states that notice of the service "shall be provided on the application ... and/or the statement of facts" — i.e., every Colorado SNAP applicant is given the opportunity to receive that non-cash service as part of applying. No supplied fact in this volume's interface captures whether a household affirmatively took up the non-cash service. The draft's `snap_meets_ece_eligibility` (CO-339) therefore tests only the 200% gross income limit and the IPV/drug-felony exclusion, treating the TANF/MOE notice-and-authorization condition as met whenever a household applies (consistent with Colorado's practice of extending the non-cash service to every applicant through the application form itself). A future revision should add a supplied fact if a case needs to model a household that was not actually authorized for the service. Items: CO-339, CO-340.

### OQ-13. Resolved: the elderly/disabled SE household is not subject to the gross income test at all

Body: 10 CCR 2506-1 4.206,C,3,b,1) (S49) and 4.401,A,3,b (S50) each state only a net-income (100% FPL) and resource requirement for an SE household with an elderly or disabled member, with no gross income figure given for that household type. This was originally treated as an assumption drawn from silence. It is not: 7 CFR 273.10(e)(2)(i)(B) (S103) states in terms that "In addition to meeting the net income eligibility standards, households which do not contain an elderly or disabled member shall have their gross income ... compared to the gross monthly income standards" -- i.e., a household that does contain an elderly or disabled member is not subject to the gross income test. snap_meets_gross_income_test (CO-347) is correct as written (deeming the test met via snap_household_has_elderly_or_disabled_member) and now cites S103 alongside S41, S49, S50. Items: CO-347.

### OQ-14. Destitute migrant/seasonal farmworker expedited-service ground modeled with a new supplied fact, not from a fetched definition of "destitute"

Body: 7 CFR 273.2(i)(1)(ii) (S58) grants expedited service to a "[m]igrant or seasonal farmworker household[] who [is] destitute as defined in § 273.10(e)(3)," which this batch's document set does not include (273.10 is not among D-1..D-25). The draft adds a supplied fact, `is_destitute_migrant_or_seasonal_farmworker` (CO-055), for a county worker's determination of that status under the unfetched 273.10(e)(3) standard, rather than trying to reconstruct the destitution test from memory. A future batch that fetches 273.10 should replace this supplied fact's `meaning` with a derived rule citing that text, if the composition of "destitute" income and resources is itself decomposable into facts this volume already has. Items: CO-055, CO-347.

### OQ-15. The 200% BBCE income limit table is dated October 1, 2024 (10 CCR 2506-1 4.401.1), a year behind the FY2026 130%/100% FNS tables used for the SE gross and net limits

Body: D-21 (S52, S53), the FNS FY2026 income eligibility standards, gives the 130% and 100% dollar figures effective October 1, 2025, and this batch versions snap_gross_income_limit_table and snap_net_income_limit_table from that date. Colorado's own codified 200% BBCE table (10 CCR 2506-1 4.401.1, S59) is the only fetched source with a stated 200% dollar figure, and it is captioned "Effective October 1, 2024" — the rule manual has evidently not been updated for FY2026 as of this drafting. snap_bbce_gross_income_limit_table (CO-122) is therefore versioned from 2024-10-01 with no `to`, one year behind its 130%/100% counterparts, which will slightly understate the 200% ceiling relative to what FNS's FY2026 cost-of-living adjustment implies. A future revision should replace this table once CDHS publishes an updated 4.401.1 or a computed 200%-of-poverty-guideline figure is confirmed as the correct FY2026 replacement. Items: CO-122, CO-123.

### OQ-31. Whether the amended ABAWD age exception ("under 18, or over 65") sets the subject-to range at 18-65 or 18-64

Body: Pub. L. 119-21 sec. 10102 (S128), amending 7 U.S.C. 2015(o)(3)(A) (S127), exempts an individual "under 18, or over 65, years of age." Read literally, "over 65" exempts only a person who has turned 66, leaving the subject-to range at 18 through 65 inclusive. The draft (`snap_abawd_max_age`, CO-166) follows that literal reading. A common alternative reading, applied in other volumes of this codex (e.g. `mwr`'s WR-121, which cites "the reported FNS implementation"), treats the exemption as reaching age 65 itself, putting the subject-to ceiling at 64. No document in this volume's set states which reading FNS or CDHS applies to the amended text; the draft's choice should be revisited once CDHS issues implementing guidance. Items: CO-166, CO-412, CO-418.

### OQ-32. The pre-H.R.1 ABAWD exceptions for homelessness, veteran status, and former foster youth are not carried into the current-law time limit rule

Body: 7 CFR 273.24(c)(7)-(9) (S131) and 10 CCR 2506-1 4.311.1.C-E (S132) still list homelessness, veteran status, and having aged out of foster care at 18 (through age 24) as ABAWD exemptions. Pub. L. 119-21 sec. 10102 (S128) struck and replaced the entire exceptions paragraph, 7 U.S.C. 2015(o)(3), with a shorter list (S127) that does not include any of the three. Per the design brief's instruction that the amended statute controls where the regulation and Colorado's rule manual have not caught up (see OQ-1's treatment of the parallel alien-eligibility conflict), the draft's `snap_is_exempt_from_abawd_time_limit` (CO-414) does not model homelessness, veteran status, or former-foster-youth status as ABAWD exemptions, even though `is_homeless`, `is_veteran_or_active_duty`, and `was_foster_youth_at_eighteen` remain in this volume's supplied-fact interface for other rules. A future revision should replace this with CDHS guidance implementing sec. 10102 if the regulation and rule manual are updated to match, or if CDHS chooses to continue these exemptions under a state option this draft has not found documented. Items: CO-414.

### OQ-33. "Otherwise exempt under subsection (d)(2)" (the ABAWD exception incorporating general work-registration exemptions) is read as coextensive with this volume's snap_is_exempt_from_work_registration

Body: 7 U.S.C. 2015(o)(3)(D) (S127, S128) exempts an ABAWD who is "otherwise exempt under subsection (d)(2)," which is the same work-registration exemption paragraph modeled at CO-405 (`snap_is_exempt_from_work_registration`), sourced from 7 CFR 273.7(b)(1) and 10 CCR 2506-1 4.310.3 rather than the statute's own (d)(2) text directly. The draft treats the regulatory and Colorado exemption lists as faithful implementations of (d)(2) and reads CO-414's reference to CO-405 as satisfying 2015(o)(3)(D), rather than re-deriving a separate (d)(2)-only fact from S121 alone. Items: CO-414.

### OQ-34. "Single parent" for the full-time-student exemption is modeled as the absence of a spouse relationship, not a count of parents in the household

Body: 7 U.S.C. 2015(e)(8) (S133) and 10 CCR 2506-1 4.306.1.D (S135) state the single-parent full-time-student exemption applies "in those situations where only one natural, adoptive, or stepparent ... is in the same SNAP household as the child." This volume's relationship facts do not let a rule count how many parents of a given child are in the household directly; the draft (`snap_is_single_parent_full_time_student_exemption`, CO-409) instead tests that the person is a parent of a child under the relevant age and has no spouse relationship recorded, which is an approximation (a household could, in principle, include a second parent who is not the person's spouse). Items: CO-409.

### OQ-35. The Federal minimum wage dollar figure is not stated in any fetched document

Body: The work-registration employment exemption (7 U.S.C. 2015(d)(1)(E), S121; 7 CFR 273.7(b)(1)(vii), S123) and the student employment exemption (7 U.S.C. 2015(e)(4), S133; 7 CFR 273.5(b)(5), S134) both reference "the minimum hourly rate under the Fair Labor Standards Act of 1938 [29 U.S.C. 206(a)(1)]" without stating the dollar amount, and no other document in this volume's set (D-1 through D-25) quotes it either. The draft's `federal_minimum_wage` parameter (CO-160) uses $7.25, the rate in effect since 2009-07-24 under 29 U.S.C. 206(a)(1), without a document citation for the number itself, consistent with the mwr volume's WR-101 parameter of the same name. A future revision should replace this with a fetched excerpt of 29 U.S.C. 206(a)(1) if the number needs a primary citation. Items: CO-160.

### OQ-22. Child support paid is an exclusion from gross income in Colorado, applied per person before proration

Body: 10 CCR 2506-1 4.407.5,A (S99) excludes legally binding child support paid to or for a non-household member from the household's total countable gross income before any gross income test, Colorado's election under 7 U.S.C. 2014(e)(4); 4.411.1,B,2,c says the payments are deducted before an ineligible member's income is prorated. Resolved in the fix round: snap_person_gross_income subtracts child_support_paid_legally_obligated (floored at zero per person), so the exclusion reaches the gross income test and the prorated share alike, and the separate child support deduction item was removed. What remains open is 4.407.5,D's three-month averaging of the payments, which the supplied monthly amount is assumed to already reflect. Items: CO-332.

### OQ-23. Which of 7 CFR 273.10(e)(2)(ii)(A)'s two rounding options for 30% of net income Colorado elected is not stated in any fetched Colorado document

Body: 7 CFR 273.10(e)(2)(ii)(A) (S102) gives State agencies two options for the cents in 30% of household net income when computing the allotment: round that figure up to the next dollar before subtracting it from the maximum allotment (option (1)), or leave it unrounded and instead round the final allotment down to the next lower dollar (option (2)). No fetched Colorado document (D-17, D-18, D-19) states which option CDHS uses. Per the design brief's instruction, snap_benefit_reduction (CO-384; not CO-382, which is snap_meets_net_income_test - N-3 fix-round correction) takes option (1) (`ceil`). In CO-01/CO-02, option (1) and option (2) reach the same $125 allotment (option (2): 785 - 659.55 = 125.45, floored to 125), so the choice is not load-bearing for the target household. Items: CO-384.

### OQ-24. The homeless shelter deduction is modeled as available only when it exceeds the household's computed excess shelter deduction, not as a household election

Body: 10 CCR 2506-1 4.407.3,C (S97) and 7 CFR 273.9(d)(6)(i) (S92) describe the homeless shelter deduction as a standard estimate available to a household in which all members are experiencing homelessness and are not receiving free shelter, in lieu of claiming actual shelter costs; the household (not the rule) chooses between the standard estimate and actual costs when actual costs are higher and verified. This volume's supplied-fact interface has no fact recording which of the two a household elected. snap_homeless_shelter_deduction (CO-380) instead takes whichever produces the larger deduction for a household in which all members are homeless (comparing the flat amount to the computed snap_excess_shelter_deduction from the household's stated shelter_cost_monthly/utility_allowance_amount), and snap_household_net_income (CO-381) subtracts the greater of the two rather than both. This approximates the household's own optimal election without a dedicated supplied fact for it. Items: CO-380, CO-381.

### OQ-25. Colorado's own 4.407.1 standard deduction table is a year behind the FY2026 table this batch uses

### OQ-26. A three-or-more-member household whose computed allotment is $0 is modeled as ineligible (ratio "zero_allotment"), not as a suspended participant

Body: 7 CFR 273.10(e)(2)(iii) (S105) gives a State agency two options for an otherwise-eligible household of three or more members whose computed allotment would be $0: deny (or, post-certification, discontinue) the household outright, or certify the household but suspend its participation pending a future change that would restore a positive allotment. No fetched Colorado document states which option CDHS elects, and this volume's interface has no supplied or derived fact for "suspended" status distinct from "ineligible." snap_eligibility_status (CO-387) and snap_is_eligible (CO-386) take the denial reading (S105(A)): a household of any size with a zero (or negative, floored at zero) allotment is treated as not eligible for the month, with snap_eligibility_status reporting "zero_allotment." Items: CO-385, CO-386, CO-387.

### OQ-27. The earned income deduction's base for a prorated ineligible non-citizen member's earned share is built as a new item rather than reading snap_household_earned_income directly

Body: 10 CCR 2506-1 4.411.1,B,2,c (S107) applies the earned income deduction to "the prorated income earned by the disqualified and/or sanctioned member that is attributed to the household," in addition to the household's own eligible members' earned income. Batch 2's snap_household_earned_income (CO-335) sums only snap_eligible_household_members' own earned income and does not include any share of an ineligible member's earned income (by design, per CO-335's own rationale). This batch cannot edit CO-335, so snap_counted_earned_income_of_ineligible_member (CO-371) and snap_household_earned_income_for_deduction (CO-372) build the earned-income-deduction base as snap_household_earned_income plus the same pro rata (or full, for a fraud/work-sanction/fleeing-felon disqualification) share of each ineligible member's own earned income that snap_counted_income_of_ineligible_member (CO-333) already computes for gross income, mirrored onto the earned-income component alone. Items: CO-371, CO-372.

### OQ-28. Proration of a disqualified member's billed shelter and dependent care expenses (4.411.1,B,2,c) is not modeled

Body: 10 CCR 2506-1 4.411.1,B,2,c (S107) also states that shelter and dependent care expenses paid by or billed to a disqualified or sanctioned member (other than the standard utility allowance, which is added in full) are prorated evenly among all household members including the disqualified member, with only the remaining members' share deductible. This volume's supplied-fact interface does not distinguish which household member a shelter or dependent care expense is billed to; snap_shelter_costs (CO-377) and snap_dependent_care_deduction (CO-374) sum shelter_cost_monthly/utility_allowance_amount and dependent_care_expense over every household member's stated value without prorating out a disqualified member's own share. This is a simplification a future revision should replace once the supplied-fact interface can attribute an expense to the member who is billed for it. Items: CO-377, CO-374.

### OQ-29. Shelter costs and the standard utility allowance are aggregated by summing every household member's own stated value, treating an unstated (unknown) value as zero rather than as missing

Body: shelter_cost_monthly and utility_allowance_amount are described in this volume's supplied-fact interface as "stated once per unit, carried on the head," but no fact or relationship identifies which member is the head, and the P39 sum pattern (docs/conventions.md) makes a sum over a group unknown if any member's value of the summed fact is unknown. snap_shelter_costs (CO-377) instead sums only over the household members whose shelter_cost_monthly/utility_allowance_amount is actually known (filtering out members for whom the fact was never asked), so a value stated on any one member (typically the head) is picked up and members who were never asked are treated as contributing zero rather than making the whole sum unknown. This is a reasonable reading given the interface, but it means a household member's shelter_cost_monthly of $0 (asked and answered) is indistinguishable in this rule from a member the interface never asked. Items: CO-377.

### OQ-30. Whether the excess shelter deduction and the net income computation floor intermediate income at $0 before applying the 50% shelter share is not addressed by the fetched text

Body: 7 CFR 273.10(e)(1)(i)(H) (S101) and 10 CCR 2506-1 4.407.3,A (S96) describe the 50% shelter-income test as applied to "the household's income after all other deductions," without addressing what happens if the standard, earned income, medical, dependent care, and child support deductions computed so far exceed gross income (a negative intermediate figure). snap_adjusted_income_before_shelter (CO-378) floors this intermediate figure at $0 before the 50% shelter share is taken, on the reading that "income after deductions" cannot itself be negative for this purpose (consistent with the final net income floor at $0 that 7 CFR 273.10(e)(1) implies throughout). Items: CO-378.

### OQ-36. The standard utility allowance is not modeled as a rule; utility_allowance_amount remains a supplied fact pending the CDHS SUA table

Body: P-1/P-2/P-3 of the fix-round review found that the standard utility allowance (SUA) is the highest-value unmodeled provision in the chapter: 7 CFR 273.9(d)(6)(iii) (S138) authorizes a heating/cooling standard (HCSUA), a limited utility allowance (LUA, which may include telephone and/or internet), and a telephone-only standard, each with its own dollar figure and its own entitlement conditions, and Colorado's CDHS annual SUA notice is not among this volume's fetched documents (D-1..D-25). This volume therefore keeps `utility_allowance_amount` (CO-036) as a supplied fact rather than inventing figures, per the provenance rule in the authoring brief. A future batch that fetches the CDHS SUA table (10 CCR 2506-1 4.407.3's cross-reference, or CDHS's annual notice) should replace it with a derived `snap_standard_utility_allowance` item that: (1) keys the HCSUA, LUA, and telephone-only dollar amounts on a parameter table sourced from that notice; (2) determines entitlement to each tier from the household's stated utility expenses (heating/cooling costs incurred, or the count of other qualifying utility costs for the LUA) rather than treating entitlement as already decided; (3) limits the LIHEAP/other-energy-assistance-triggered HCSUA to households with an elderly or disabled member, per Pub. L. 119-21 sec. 10103 (S136), which amended 7 U.S.C. 2014(e)(6)(C)(iv)(I) and 2014(k)(4) to add that condition; and (4) excludes internet connection service fees from the LUA/shelter computation, per Pub. L. 119-21 sec. 10104 (S137), which added new 7 U.S.C. 2014(e)(6)(E) ("Any service fee associated with internet connection shall not be used in computing the excess shelter expense deduction"). In the meantime, CO-036's and CO-035's meanings have been corrected (fix-round P-2/P-3) to state that a worker supplying these amounts must exclude internet service fees, so the supplied figure is at least consistent with sec. 10104 even though no rule yet derives it. Items: CO-035, CO-036.

### OQ-37. The elderly/disabled separate-household election (7 CFR 273.1(b)(2)) is computed but not wired into household composition

Body: P-5 of the fix-round review found that the 273.1(b)(2) separate-household election (S20) had unused parameters (CO-105, CO-114) and an unused supplied fact (CO-019, is_elderly_disabled_unable_to_prepare_meals). This fix round adds `snap_meets_elderly_disabled_separate_household_election` (CO-419), which computes the income test the election turns on. It does not, however, change `snap_household_members` (CO-305) to actually carve the electing member (and spouse) out of the unit they live with: doing so needs a two-pass or fixed-point composition rule, because CO-305's buys_prepares_with/spouse/parent-child/caretaker-dependent closure would need to already know whether the election succeeds (which depends on "the others'" income, which in turn depends on who "the others" are, i.e., on composition) before it can decide whether to include or exclude the electing member. A future batch should either (a) compute CO-419 from the physical residence-sharing group (as it does now, which is composition-independent) and then have CO-305 read it as one more edge-exclusion condition (removing the buys_prepares_with/spouse edge to an electing member's non-spouse housemates when CO-419 is true for that member), or (b) restructure CO-305 as an explicit two-pass rule. Items: CO-419, CO-305.

### OQ-38. Initial-month proration, the ABAWD additional three months and regaining eligibility, and voluntary quit are out of this phase's scope

Body: The fix-round review (P-4, P-7, P-8) identified three provisions that determine eligibility or amount and are not modeled in this chapter, and this fix round does not model them (out of scope for this phase; recorded here so a future phase note can point to it):
- P-4: initial-month proration of the allotment from the date of application (7 CFR 273.10(a)(1)(ii)-(iii)) and the $10 minimum-issuance floor in an initial month, plus the initial-month carve-out from the ordinary minimum allotment (7 CFR 273.10(e)(2)(ii)(C), "[e]xcept during an initial month"). This chapter has no fact distinguishing an application date from the determination date, and no notion of an "initial month" at all.
- P-7: the ABAWD second set of up to three additional countable months (7 CFR 273.24(b), (e)) and regaining eligibility by working 80 hours in a 30-day period (7 CFR 273.24(d)). `snap_abawd_time_limit_status` (CO-418)'s enum (not_subject | within_limit | limit_reached) has no branch for either.
- P-8: voluntary quit and reduction of work effort below 30 hours a week without good cause (7 CFR 273.7(a)(1)(vii)) as an independent ground of ineligibility, distinct from the sanction-procedure fact `is_disqualified_for_work_requirement_noncompliance` (CO-040) this chapter already carries.

Each is a genuine gap for a household that applies mid-month, accrues ABAWD countable months, or has a work-quit history, but building any of the three correctly needs new facts and, for P-7, a redesign of the ABAWD counting-months items (CO-416/CO-417/CO-418) this fix round did not have the scope to redo alongside the correctness fixes (C-1..C-10) and the household-composition and case work. A future batch should model each from the citations above. Items: CO-040, CO-418.

### OQ-41. MAGI non-filer household: siblings under 19 not modeled (RESOLVED, batch 6)

Body: 42 CFR 435.603(f)(3)(iii) and 10 CCR 2505-10 8.100.4.E.1.d.iv include, for an applicant under 19, "the applicant's ... siblings under age 19" living with them. Batch 5 could not derive this: testing whether two different persons (the applicant and a candidate sibling) share a common parent looked like a two-hop join the pattern catalog's group operators could not express within one filter pass, and the one operator that walks parent/child edges, `reachable`, reapplies the same crossing condition at every hop with no notion of the original subject, over-including an intermediate minor parent's own parent when tested against p5 (whose only parent, p4, is herself under 19).

Resolution: the engine already implements a pattern batch 5 did not find - `shared_relative` (P43 in docs/conventions.md, "the persons who share a [relationship] with this person"), added for this phase and confirmed present in `src/engine/evaluate.ts`/`check.ts`/`render.ts`. It computes siblings directly (the persons, other than this person, who have a person in the named role - `parent` - in common), without walking a chain at all, so it does not over-include.
