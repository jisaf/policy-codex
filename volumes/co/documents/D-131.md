I. Background
Implementation of the One Big Beautiful
Bill Act of 2025
On July 4, 2025, President Trump
signed Public Law 119–21, the One Big
Beautiful Bill Act of 2025 (OBBB).
Section 10102 of the OBBB makes
significant changes to the time limit
(also known as a work requirement) for
certain Supplemental Nutrition
Assistance Program (SNAP)
participants. These changes require
more SNAP participants to work as a
condition of eligibility. The Department
is issuing this proposed rule to
implement those OBBB provisions, as
well as strengthen standards for requests
for waivers of the time limit and set
clear expectations that those who can
work, do.
The OBBB provisions build on a
longstanding and evolving
Congressional directive that SNAP must
support work. Work requirements were
first implemented as a condition of
SNAP eligibility in 1971 (Pub. L. 91–
671). In 1985, Congress required State
agencies to operate an employment and
training program (SNAP E&T) which
helps SNAP participants gain skills that
increase their employability and help
meet employers’ skilled workforce
needs. In 1996, the Personal

Additional Proposed Changes
Restricting the Definition of Area for
Waivers of the Time Limit
As the Act does not define areas
eligible for waivers of the time limit, the
Department seeks to clarify the
definition of area for waivers in
regulation. Current rules at 7 CFR
273.24(f)(6) allow State agencies nearly
unfettered discretion to define the
geographic area included in waiver
requests, which has led State agencies to
create waiver areas that maximize the
waiver’s coverage as opposed to
focusing on areas with the greatest
unemployment. State agencies have
used the flexibility of the definition of
area to pick data that best expand
waiver coverage rather than data that
best reflect actual unemployment.
Congress has recognized States have
intentionally reduced enforcement of
the SNAP work requirements through
expansive waivers, leaving
approximately 40 percent of these workready individuals under a waiver at the
time of passage of the OBBB (H. Rept.
119–106 p.47). The Department
maintains the need to tighten waiver
standards to assist low-income adults in
obtaining employment to increase their
earnings and move towards selfsufficiency, consistent with SNAP’s
program purpose. Therefore, the
Department proposes to establish
reasonable, straightforward parameters
by defining an ‘‘area’’ at 7 CFR
273.24(f)(5) to mean a Commuting Zone,
county or county-equivalent, reservation
area or a U.S. Territory.
Additionally, under current
regulatory flexibilities, State agencies
