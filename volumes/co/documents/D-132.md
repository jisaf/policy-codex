ACTION: Proposed rule.
SUMMARY: Under section 5(a) of the Food

and Nutrition Act of 2008, households
in which each member receives benefits
under a State program funded by
Temporary Assistance for Needy
Families (TANF) are categorically
eligible for the Supplemental Nutrition
Assistance Program (SNAP). Current
SNAP regulations consider TANF
benefits to include both cash assistance
and non-cash or in-kind benefits or
services from TANF-funded programs.
This proposal would limit categorical
eligibility to only households that
receive TANF assistance, as defined at
45 CFR 260.31. These revisions would
ensure that SNAP benefits are targeted
to needy households that have qualified
for TANF assistance while still aligning
categorical eligibility’s statutory
purpose of simplifying program
administration.

Prior Rulemaking
The U.S. Department of Agriculture
(USDA or the Department) previously
proposed changes to categorical
eligibility on July 24, 2019. The
proposed rule, titled ‘‘Revision of
Categorical Eligibility in the
Supplemental Nutrition Assistance
Program (SNAP),’’ would have limited
the Temporary Assistance for Needy
Families (TANF) benefits that may be
used to confer categorical eligibility for
SNAP to those that were ongoing and
substantial (84 FR 35570). Categorical
eligibility allows households that have
been determined eligible for TANF or
certain other means-tested programs to
also be eligible for SNAP. The Biden
Administration withdrew the proposed
rule on June 10, 2021 (86 FR 30795).
The Department is now proposing
revised changes to categorical eligibility
rules to limit categorical eligibility only
to households that receive TANF
assistance, as defined at 45 CFR 260.31.
As explained in the Regulatory Impact
Analysis accompanying this rule, the
Department acknowledges these
revisions may result in some level of
increased administrative burden on
States, a reduction in SNAP benefit
spending, and associated effects on
school meals eligibility.
