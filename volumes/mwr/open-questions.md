# Open Questions: Work Requirements Volume

Interpretation questions the AI first pass could not settle from the text. Each carries the assumption the draft uses so the codex stays evaluable. An engineer implements the assumption and flags the item; only a change set resolves the question. Shared by both approaches.

### OQ-1 Age during a month
A person who turns 18, 14, 6, or 65 in the middle of a month: which age applies to that month? Assumption: the age on the first day of the month, so the person is the younger age for the whole month. Items: WR-004.

### OQ-2 Postpartum entitlement is supplied, not derived
Entitlement to postpartum medical assistance depends on the pregnancy end date and the State's election of 12-month postpartum coverage under section 1902(e)(16). Assumption: the pregnancy coverage determination supplies a yes/no. A later volume should derive it. Items: WR-006.

### OQ-3 Which children "rely on another individual for care"
The interim final rule defines a dependent child as a child 13 or under who relies on another individual for care, while 42 CFR 435.4 defines dependent child by deprivation of parental support. Assumption: the community engagement subpart's definition governs, and reliance on care is a separate supplied finding rather than presumed for every child 13 or under. Items: WR-011, WR-201.

### OQ-4 Who determines "disabled individual" under the ADA definition
The rule adopts 28 CFR 35.108 and says program eligibility on the basis of disability is not required. Assumption: an eligibility worker records a yes/no from documentation or statement. Items: WR-012.

### OQ-5 Is the medically frail chapeau a separate test
42 CFR 435.554(c)(5) reads as "whose condition significantly impairs the individual's ability to comply ... and is an individual" in one of five categories. Assumption: both are required, so a person in a category whose condition does not significantly impair compliance is not medically frail. Items: WR-021, WR-206.

### OQ-6 The inmate window ends on the first day of the month
42 CFR 435.553(b) covers "the 3-month period ending on the first day of that month." Assumption: the three whole months before the month; a stay that touches only the first day of the current month is not captured at monthly granularity. Items: WR-025, WR-207.

### OQ-7 Is "work program" the same activity in both programs
Medicaid counts participation in "a work program"; SNAP counts "a work program" under 6(o)(2)(B) and workfare separately. Assumption: one supplied fact for work program hours serves both programs. Items: WR-042.

### OQ-8 Weekly averaging for the SNAP employment exemption
6(d)(2)(E) is stated per week. Assumption: monthly hours and income are converted to weekly by multiplying by 12 and dividing by 52. FNS may prescribe a different conversion. Items: WR-045, WR-304, WR-305.

### OQ-9 Waiver coverage is looked up by address
Post-H.R.1 area waivers require an unemployment rate over 10 percent (1.5 times the national rate in noncontiguous States). Assumption: waiver coverage is supplied by geography lookup outside this volume. Items: WR-051.

### OQ-10 State elections for lookback months
The State specifies 1 to 3 consecutive months before application and 1 or more months between determinations. Assumption: 1 and 1. Items: WR-105, WR-106.

### OQ-11 Implementation date
The Federal date is January 1, 2027. A State may implement earlier or hold a good-faith exemption until December 31, 2028. Assumption: January 1, 2027. Items: WR-107.

### OQ-12 Short-term hardship election and effect
The hardship exception is a State option. The statute says the State may treat the individual as having met the requirement for the month. Assumption: the State elects it, and a hardship month counts as satisfied. Items: WR-113, WR-222, WR-223.

### OQ-13 SNAP ABAWD upper age
7 U.S.C. 2015(o)(3)(A) as amended reads "under 18, or over 65, years of age." Read literally, only persons 66 and older are exempt. The reported FNS implementation treats persons 65 and older as exempt. The historical reading of "over N" in this paragraph has been implemented both ways (the former "over 50" as 50 and older; the former "over 54" as 55 and older). Assumption: 65 and older are exempt. Separately, the regulation's cross-reference to the general work registration age cap (60 and older) has not been updated, so persons 60 through 64 are treated as subject under the statute. Items: WR-121, WR-300.

### OQ-14 Clock method and months accrued before enactment
The State may use a fixed or rolling 36-month clock. Assumption: rolling. Whether countable months accrued under prior law before July 4, 2025 continue to count is not addressed in the text retrieved. Items: WR-131, WR-314.

### OQ-15 Effective date of the SNAP changes
Section 10102 states no effective date, so the changes took effect on enactment, July 4, 2025. The FNS implementation memorandum could not be retrieved; it reportedly confirms this and describes a variance exclusion period. Items: WR-132.

### OQ-16 Residence requirement for the parent or caretaker exclusion
The statute excludes a "parent, guardian, caretaker relative, or family caregiver" of a dependent child or disabled individual without a residence condition; the rule's residence and hours criteria attach only to family caregivers. Assumption: no residence condition for parents, guardians, or caretaker relatives. Items: WR-203.

### OQ-17 Which relationships make someone a "relative"
42 CFR 435.554(c)(3)(i)(B) covers a relative who does not reside with the person. Assumption: any recorded relationship (parent, guardian, caretaker relative, spouse, or relative) qualifies. Items: WR-008, WR-204.

### OQ-18 Unknown recovery start date
Assumption: a person with a substance use disorder and no recorded recovery start date is not in stable recovery. Items: WR-017, WR-205.

### OQ-19 "Not exempt from a work requirement under such Act"
Medicaid exclusion (VI)(bb) applies to a SNAP household member who is not exempt from "a work requirement" under the Food and Nutrition Act. Assumption: subject to either general work registration or the ABAWD time limit qualifies, and the statute does not require the person to be complying with it, only to be subject to it. Consequence, confirmed by case C-04: a SNAP recipient who is subject to SNAP work rules is excluded from Medicaid community engagement regardless of hours worked. Items: WR-208, WR-319.

### OQ-20 Combining education hours
The rule forbids combining educational program hours with other activities when the person is enrolled at least half-time. Assumption: below half-time, education hours combine; at or above half-time, enrollment alone demonstrates engagement and hours are not added. Items: WR-214.

### OQ-21 "Responsibility for a dependent child" in SNAP
Assumption: the person is a parent of, or provides regular care to, a child who is a member of the same SNAP household. Items: WR-302, WR-303.

### OQ-22 Good cause hours
The regulation treats a person who "would have fulfilled the work requirement but missed some hours for good cause" as fulfilling it. Assumption: hours missed for good cause are added to hours worked. Items: WR-047, WR-311.

### OQ-23 Absent activity data
Derivations that sum activity hours treat an unreported activity as 0 hours through an explicit `otherwise 0`. The alternative is to leave the month unknown until every activity is verified. Items: WR-214, WR-215, WR-304, WR-311.
