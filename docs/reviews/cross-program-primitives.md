# Cross-program primitives: what the Colorado programs share, and whether they should

Read-only review of the Colorado volume (`volumes/co`) at branch `ccr-9d19bf4c-wmnwcl`, head 3cf4b70 (2026-11-24). Reviewer role: cross-program policy analyst. The question is whether the facts and items that several programs read are true primitives, or clean derivations from true primitives, or whether they carry one program's interpretation. Nothing in the volume was changed. The companion review `docs/reviews/service-design-fact-burden.md` (FB-nn) covers the same facts from the burden side, and this review does not repeat its findings. The earlier `docs/reviews/cross-program-audit.md`, `docs/reviews/audit-same-word-different-meaning.md` and `docs/reviews/duplicate-paths-audit.md` (within-program duplicates) are also assumed.

## Summary for the product owner

The shared layer is mostly sound. Of the 272 items that two or more programs' outcomes read, 164 are true primitives (dates, amounts, records, legal statuses, published figures) and 35 are clean derivations from them (age, siblings, marriage, the 8 U.S.C. 1641 status). 10 are program items read across a declared link. The other 63 are grey. 39 of the grey items are verification flags and verified-status items: one "document received" flag per element serves up to seven programs, although each program requires different documents and periods. The remainder are a few facts and derived items that look neutral but carry one program's interpretation, and those produce wrong answers today. Five are shown with the engine:

- `state_of_residence` builds in the "intends to remain" test of Colorado Works and Adult Financial. A seasonal worker in Colorado with a job is therefore a non-resident for Medicaid, although 42 CFR 435.403 makes him a resident.
- `lives_with` is a value of the determination date. MAGI Medicaid reads it as "lived together in the month" and ignores the separation date that SNAP and the SSI method honour. A husband who lived in the home all of October drops out of his wife's October MAGI household, and she becomes eligible.
- The shared military connection treats any unmarried child of a veteran as a "dependent child", at any age. A stored household (CO-154) already shows SNAP and Medicaid disagreeing about the same 41-year-old.
- The receipt field `irregular` carries the SSI definition of "infrequent", and SNAP and CCCAP read it as their own different test.
- The verification flags decide four programs' pending status at once.

Dependence between programs is concentrated in three places:

- Medicaid's community-engagement exemption and the community spouse's utility allowance pull 79 SNAP items into 15 of Medicaid's 35 outcomes.
- The Adult Financial bar for Colorado Works pulls 86 Colorado Works items into 7 Adult Financial outcomes, including the Old Age Pension grant of a 72-year-old couple with no children.
- CHP+ legitimately adopts Medicaid's MAGI method, and through it also inherits the SNAP chain.

Each of the three can be replaced by a case-record fact or a primitive gate.

For sharing more, the main opportunity is ages and dates. The three month-age items are neutral date arithmetic filed under Medicaid's prefix, so SNAP, Colorado Works, Adult Financial and SUN Bucks write the same arithmetic inline 70 times. "Pregnant in the month" and "lived together in the month" each exist in three program copies that already disagree.

The proposed rule is that a concept is shared only when it can be documented without any program's rule, no program's text defines it differently, and it is dated wherever a monthly rule reads it. It is derived by date logic, arithmetic or relationship closure only. Anything that fails is split into a shared primitive plus program-local readings.

## Method

1. **Read graph.** The script `scratch-xp/graph.ts` uses the codex's own loader and `src/engine/graph.ts` (`buildGraph`, the same dependency walk the change-impact check uses). It loads all 2,422 items: 760 supplied, 1,182 derived and 480 parameters. Its edges are every reference in a derivation, with parameters and the determination date included.
2. **Outcomes and program families.** There are 159 outcome items (`role: outcome`). Each is assigned to one of the brief's eight families:
   - SNAP
   - MAGI Medicaid with CHP+ (MAGI_CHP)
   - non-MAGI Medicaid (NonMAGI)
   - Adult Financial (AF)
   - Colorado Works (CW)
   - CCCAP
   - LEAP
   - SUN Bucks

   Medicaid outcomes are split by their tags: `magi` goes to MAGI_CHP, and `non_magi`, `ltc`, `msp`, `buy_in` and `oap` go to NonMAGI. The 18 Medicaid outcomes with neither tag (verification, emergency Medicaid, BCCP, needy young person) form a Medicaid-wide group.
3. **Reach.** An item's *own reach* is the set of families whose outcomes read it. The walk descends only through items of the outcome's own program and shared (All) items. Another program's derived item is recorded as read, but its inputs stay that program's. *Transitive reach* descends through everything and measures dependence. A **shared item** is one reached by two or more families. Medicaid's own items whose reach stays inside Medicaid are excluded, which leaves 272 items: 255 program-All items and 17 owned by a program.
4. **Rubric.** `scratch-xp/classify.py` applies the rubric below to every shared item. Default classes are set by kind and type, with an override, its tests and its reason for each item read by hand. Table 1 lists them all.
5. **Probes.** For each claim that an answer is wrong, a stored household was varied and evaluated through `src/engine/evaluate.ts`. The runner is `scratch-xp/probe.ts` and the probe files are `scratch-xp/p-*.yaml`. `scratch-xp/cases_scan.py` counts the stored households that each proposal touches.
6. **Paragraphs.** Every quoted excerpt is from `volumes/co/documents/D-*.md` or `volumes/co/sources.md`.

The scripts are scratch work in the worktree's untracked `scratch-xp/` folder and are not part of the volume. The per-item reach is in `docs/reviews/cross-program-primitives.json`, with records of the form `{id, identifier, kind, home_program, read_by_programs[], class}` plus the program field, transitive reach and the cross-program edges.

## The rubric: when a concept is shareable

### Business rule

> A concept may be shared across programs when it is a **primitive** or a **clean derivation**. It is a primitive when it passes T1–T3. It is a clean derivation when it is derived and passes T1–T4. A concept that fails T1 is **program-local**: it carries the program's prefix, or it is a finding of the program that makes it (conventions rule 13(i)). A concept that passes T1 but fails T2 or T3 is **grey**. It is shared only after it is split into a primitive that passes all four, plus one program-local reading per program whose paragraph treats it differently.
>
> One exception is an **adopted method**: the SSI resource method, the Revenue Appendix A citizenship documents, or CHP+'s use of the MAGI household. It may be shared with the programs whose own text adopts it, and with no others, under conventions "Cross-program links". It is listed as grey (adopted) so that the adopter list stays visible.

| Test | Question | Passes when | Fails when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1 Observable** | Can a caseworker document or observe it without applying any program's rule? | It is a date, an amount, a document, a record of another agency or case, a relationship stated in legal or factual terms, or a published figure. | Answering it needs a program's definition: "countable", "household", "disabled for X", "verified", "good cause", "intends to remain" as a residency test. |
| **T2 Not redefined** | Does any reading program's paragraph define the concept differently, or add a condition the shared item does not test? | Every reader applies the item as written, or the one program that differs reads its own item. | Two readers' texts differ, and both read the shared item. |
| **T3 Stable** | Is one value right for every period a reader reads it for? | It cannot change within a month, or it is dated so that each reader can take its own day (birth, entry, separation, start and end dates). | It is a value of the determination date that a monthly rule reads for other months, or an "as of now" status with no date. |
| **T4 Mechanical** | (Derived items) Is it date logic, arithmetic or relationship closure only? | `years_between`, sums, filters over stated edges, a statute's list applied to a stated status. | It decides something its inputs do not state: an absence, a dependency, a sufficiency. |

**Grey zone in practice.** Three patterns cover every grey item found:

- **Definition in the name.** The item's meaning quotes a statute term that its derivation does not test, for example "dependent" in XP-3.
- **Lump.** One number or flag stands for a list that programs filter differently: medical expenses, documents received (XP-5, XP-6).
- **Snapshot read as a month.** A determination-date value is read for other months: `lives_with`, `age`, `resides_in_medical_institution` (XP-2, XP-9).

The fix is always the same: share the dated, itemised, observable part, and let each program read it through its own item.

## Keep as is: sharing that is right

- **Dates of events** (`date_of_birth`, `date_of_death`, `pregnancy_start_date`, `pregnancy_end_date`, `institution_entry_date`, `us_entry_date`, `qualified_status_date`, `first_qualified_status_date`, the separation dates). Each program picks the day it needs. This is the strongest part of the design and the model for XP-1, XP-2 and XP-9.
- **Relationship edges and their closures** (`siblings`, `half_siblings`, `step_siblings`, `full_siblings`, `is_related_to`, `is_married`, `sponsors`, `sponsor_spouses_not_cosigning`). The roles name legal or factual relationships (rule 16). No household is shared, and each program builds its own from the edges. The one interpretive role worth watching is `has_primary_responsibility_for`. SNAP reads it for "parental control", which 10 CCR 2506-1 defines as being "financially or otherwise dependent on that individual member" (D-82), while Colorado Works and MAGI read it as assumed care. A financially dependent child with no stated care edge falls between the two (CO-018 precision). That is grey, not a finding yet.
- **Records with one counting table per program** (`receipts`, `earnings`, `holdings`). Program findings on holdings carry their prefix (`snap_trust_inaccessible`, `ma_found_available_value`, `af_found_unsaleable`). The exceptions are the `irregular` field (XP-4) and the holdings field `accessible`, whose reasons field (`inaccessible_reason`) lets the SSI method filter while SNAP reads it bare. That is acceptable while SNAP's 4.410 inaccessible list covers every reason; re-check when a reason is added.
- **Document type facts with acceptability per program** (`citizenship_verification_document_type`, `identity_verification_document_type`, and `snap_`/`ma_`/`af_citizenship_document_is_acceptable`). This is the pattern XP-5 proposes for every verification element.
- **One immigration status and the statute's own categories** (`immigration_status`, `is_qualified_non_citizen`, `is_exempt_from_five_year_bar`, `has_held_qualified_status_five_years`, `meets_public_assistance_citizenship_requirement`). Each program's eligible list is its own item.
- **Neutral setting facts** (`residence_setting_type` in 42 CFR 435.1010's terms, `nighttime_residence_type`, `temporary_absence_reason`). Each program's institution, homelessness and absence rules are local (`snap_is_homeless`, `sebt_is_homeless_child`, `cw_is_considered_living_in_home`).
- **Agency findings stated as findings** (`meets_ssa_disability_criteria`, `meets_nursing_facility_level_of_care`, `title_ii_benefit_type`, `va_disability_rating_type`, the case-status records `receives_ssi`, `receives_tanf_cash`, `complies_with_tanf_work_requirements`). FB-05 covers the overlap of the receipt-status facts with the receipt records.
- **Published figures** (`federal_poverty_guideline_annual_table`, the SSI figures, `snap_maximum_allotment_table` read for boarders). Each program chooses the version in force from its own rule (`as_of`).
- **CHP+ adopting Medicaid's MAGI household and income** (42 adopted-method reads). This is required by 42 CFR 457.310 and 10 CCR 2505-3, and is correct. The only cost is the SNAP chain it inherits, which XP-7(a) removes.
- **`is_full_time_secondary_student_finishing_before_nineteen`**: Medicaid (42 CFR 435.4) and Colorado Works (3.601) state the test in the same words, and the item's meaning names both.
