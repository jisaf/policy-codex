# Service design review: the burden of the Colorado fact structure

Read-only review of the Colorado volume (`volumes/co`) at branch `ccr-9d19bf4c-wmnwcl`, head 77fe49a (2026-11-24). Reviewer role: public-sector service designer. Question: where does the way the codex structures supplied facts and record fields put work on applicants, caseworkers or intake systems that the governing rule does not ask for? Nothing in the volume was changed.

## Summary for the product owner

The codex asks for far more than its rules need, and most of the excess is in questions whose answer is almost always "no". Decision 4 ("missing information is cannot determine unless the rule gives a default") has been applied fact by fact. It has not been applied to the shape of the rules themselves. Many rules frame a condition as something the household claims (a disability benefit, a deduction), something an authority finds (a fraud disqualification, a continuous-eligibility exception, a "questionable" statement) or an exception to the normal case. Each of those framings carries its own default, but the codex treats them as facts every person must state. The 548 stored households show the effect. 73 supplied facts are required (leaving them out gives "cannot determine") in at least 10 households, yet hold a non-default value in fewer than 10% of them, for 3,767 required statements in all. 30 of those facts are required in 50 or more households. Examples: the four "other MAGI income" amounts are required in 102 households and are non-zero in none; six SNAP disability-benefit categories are required in 87–91 households and hold a non-default value in 0–7; pregnancy is asked of every member, men and infants included, and is true in 15 of 199 households. In a simulation where an intake system leaves the "no / none / 0" answers to these rare questions blank, 478 of 546 households lose at least one expected answer and 412 lose a program outcome. The fixes here change no stored answer, because they replace the stated "no" with the rule's own default. They would remove roughly half of what a typical household has to state (median 105 stated facts per household, 54 per person). Two further groups need policy decisions, not just defaults: about 115 supplied facts that no rule reads are still on the interface, and benefit status is stated twice (as a status fact and again as a receipt record), with disagreements already visible in the stored data.

## Method and evidence

1. **Inventory.** I walked every derived item's expression tree to count, for each supplied fact and each record field, its readers and whether the reader gives an absence meaning (`otherwise` / `unknown`). 760 supplied facts: 418 yes/no, 154 money, 78 dates, 46 one-of. 115 of them have no reader. Record fields: receipts (CO-5000) 34, earnings (CO-5100) 36, holdings (CO-5300) 64.
2. **Stated vs read vs required vs value.** I ran the engine over all 548 stored households (`volumes/co/tests/cases.yaml`) and evaluated every expectation with a trace. For each fact a household states, I recorded:
   - whether any expectation reads it;
   - whether dropping it turns any expectation into cannot determine ("required");
   - whether changing its value moves any expectation;
   - whether the stated value is non-default (true, non-zero or not `none`).
3. **Blank-the-noes simulation.** For each group of facts in the findings, I deleted every stated value that is false, 0 or `none` and counted the expectations that become cannot determine. If each fact took the rule's default instead, the same run would reproduce every stored answer exactly. That is the basis for "no stored answer moves" in the risk column.
4. **Rule reading.** For each top candidate I read the excerpt the item cites (`volumes/co/sources.md`, `volumes/co/documents/D-*.md`).

The measurement scripts were scratch work (built on the earlier `verify-closeout/probe.ts` runner) and are not committed. Each number below can be reproduced by evaluating the stored households with and without the named fact.

**Caveat.** The stored households are test fixtures chosen to exercise edge cases. Rare conditions are over-represented in them compared with a real caseload, so the non-default rates below overstate how often the rare facts matter.

Per-person and per-household load in the stored households: median 54 stated facts per person (90th percentile 136), median 105 per household (90th percentile 282, max 1,037).

## Patterns and design principles

1. **Ask at the precision the rule decides at.** The rules compare with thresholds: 180 days, 5 hours a week, 22 August 1996, "above or at or below the applicable standard" (42 CFR 435.952(c)(1)). Ask the comparison (FB-10, FB-11). Ask for an exact figure only where the rule computes with it.
2. **Ask the rule's trigger, not its taxonomy.** The SNAP disability definition is a list of things a person *receives*; the Indian definition is a list of legal categories; the five-year bar runs from a document's class of admission. Ask the plain-language trigger, then take the category from a document or a data source (FB-01, FB-11, FB-15).
3. **A finding someone else makes is a record, not a question, and its absence means "no finding".** Fraud disqualifications, continuous-eligibility exceptions, "questionable" statements, an SSA disability determination and a child-welfare referral exist only once an authority makes them, so absence is the rule's own default (FB-04, FB-06, FB-14). Decision 4 should be read with this in mind: a rule that states a condition as a finding, a claim or an exception *does* give a default.
4. **Screen at the household, detail at the person.** "Is anyone pregnant / away / in foster care / on strike?" asked once replaces one statement per member (FB-03, FB-07, FB-08).
5. **One statement, one place.** Derive status from the records already held (receives SSI from an SSI receipt), retire scalars the records replaced, and never keep two facts for one question (two drug-felony facts, two other-state-cash facts, one VA amount in two fields) (FB-05, FB-10, FB-13, FB-17).
6. **"Required where read" means read on the path that matters.** Evaluation order is a burden decision. Test the condition that makes a rare fact relevant (has a parent in the household, is in long-term care, has used TANF months) before reading the rare fact (FB-12, FB-16, FB-10).
7. **One absence meaning per fact, stated once and honoured by every reader.** If programs differ, the difference must be a cited rule. A default written in a fact's text and ignored by its readers is a defect (FB-06, FB-11).
8. **Claims default to unclaimed; income never defaults.** A deduction, an expense or an exemption the household asserts is not taken when it is not asserted (7 CFR 273.2(f)(3)(ii)). Income and resources keep "cannot determine" (FB-09, FB-02's attestation).
9. **Retire what nothing reads.** A supplied fact with no reader is still a question on someone's form (FB-13).

## Burden the rules genuinely require (do not simplify)

- **`date_of_birth` as an exact date.** Ages are compared at many thresholds, per month and per program; changing it moved an answer in 443 of the 461 households where it is required.
- **`immigration_status` (the full documented category set) and its verification.** 42 CFR 435.945(a) carves it out of attestation: "Except where the law requires other procedures (such as for citizenship and immigration status information)". See also 7 CFR 273.2(f)(1)(ii). Keep `qualified_status_date` for non-citizens in their first five years; FB-11 changes how the prior status is obtained, not whether.
- **SNAP gross non-exempt income, verified at application.** 7 CFR 273.2(f)(1)(i): "Gross nonexempt income shall be verified for all households prior to certification."
- **Dated pays where a rule counts by date.** LEAP's eight weeks before application (9 CCR 2503-7 3.752.22), CCCAP's prior thirty days, SNAP income after the application date, and the per-job `job` label LEAP averages by.
- **`seasonal_or_commission` on each wage record.** MAGI annualizes that pay (10 CCR 2505-10 8.100.4.C.4.a, S3356). One yes/no per job.
- **Tax-filing relationships and `is_claimed_by_filer_outside_case`.** 8.100.4.E.1 has them "established as declared on the Single Streamlined Application", which asks them of each person. The fact is required in 89 households and true in none, but the application's own question is the rule's source.
- **Holdings `value` / `amount_owed`, and disposals with dates in the look-back.** Resource tests (Adult Financial, the Medicare Savings Programs, non-MAGI, SNAP outside broad-based categorical eligibility) and the 60-month transfer look-back (42 U.S.C. 1396p(c)) compute with them.
- **Application and determination dates** (`snap_application_date`, `ma_application_date`, `leap_application_date`) and the per-program application/renewal type. They set proration, the retroactive window and the renewal rules, and a household can be applying to one program while renewing another.
- **Expense amounts the household does claim.** Shelter, dependent care, medical, and the legal obligation for child support paid (7 CFR 273.2(f)(1)(xii) requires verifying the obligation and the amount paid). FB-09 changes only what an *unclaimed* expense means.
- **`residence_setting_type` for the person who is not in a private home.** Institution status decides eligibility in every program; changing it moved an answer in 326 of 327 households. FB-08 asks it as a household screener, but the answer for the person in a facility stays exact.
- **`pregnancy_start_date` for retroactive months.** Already conditional: read only when retroactive coverage is requested (CO-3600).
- **`months_of_assistance_received` (the 60-month TANF clock, 3.606.6.A).** It is a count the rule computes with. It belongs in the agency's tracking system, not on the application.
