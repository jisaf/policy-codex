## 2. New required facts and common households

- **previous_pregnancy_start_date (CO-7840).** It is read only for a month on or before the end of an earlier recorded pregnancy, for a person pregnant now. Common households do not reach it:
  - D2, a new applicant pregnant again: every month the application covers is after the earlier end, and the months before coverage answer ineligible_before_coverage_period without it.
  - D3, a postpartum mother with an older child: the stated start governs.
  - CO-1123, a renewal: the determination month and the months of its coverage period are determined without it.
  - Only a month of the earlier pregnancy itself asks for it (D1b, CO-1151), which is the fact the rule needs. **No common household moves to cannot determine.**
- **has_lived_apart_throughout_months_evaluated (CO-7860).** Unstated, it reads as false and the separation date is required, as before the fix (G3: October cannot determine before and after). The determination month does not read it (G1 to G3: November $562). **No household moves to cannot determine.** One rare household cannot state it (F3).
- **The non_qualified_status option (CO-2700).** Medicaid reads it as V7-8 intends (L1). But the start of the waiting period (CO-7820) reads only none as "the permanent residence is the first qualified status". So a lawful permanent resident inside five years of the permanent residence who correctly states non_qualified_status is cannot determine for SNAP (A3), Colorado Works (B4) and Adult Financial (B5), unless the case states the permanent-residence date a second time as first_qualified_status_date (A3d, B4w, B5w). The rule gives the answer from the option alone. That is a common household: an adult who adjusted from a student, work, fiancé or visitor visa, in a mixed-status SNAP or Colorado Works case. **F1.**
