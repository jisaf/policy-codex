## 1. FS-MA-3 / OQ-1348: a veterans benefit's part for a dependent, the SSI method (fixed, no new operator)

**Text.** 20 CFR 416.1123(e)(2) (S4756, D-53): "If you are a dependent of an individual who receives a veterans benefit and a portion of the benefit is attributable to you as a dependent, we count the amount attributable to you as your unearned cash income if (i) You reside with the individual who receives the veterans benefit, or (ii) You receive your own separate payment from the Department of Veterans Affairs." (e)(1) was already read on the veteran's side.

**Wrong answer.** The part left the veteran's figure and reached no one: the wife of a veteran paid $1,500 with $300 attributable to her had SSI-method unearned income 0, by hand 300.

**Why no operator was needed.** OQ-1348 said a condition inside a sum over the veteran's list could not compare the record's person field with the person evaluated. It can: `["of", ["P"], receipts]` binds that person to the list's owner and leaves this person unchanged, and the field sweep fix round had already compared `for_person` with this person as `["in_group", ["receipt", "for_person"], ["reachable", ["spouse"], false]]` (the persons joined to this person by no edge: the person alone) in Adult Financial's and Colorado Works' reading of the same sentence (CO-5017, CO-5651). The idiom is now written down for engine teams (docs/operators.md, "A record on another person's list that names the person evaluated").

**Fix.** ma_ssi_unearned_receipts (CO-5511), ma_ssi_gross_unearned_receipts (CO-5514) and ma_ssi_irregular_unearned_receipts (CO-6730) add, for the dependent, the `for_dependent` of each receipt the item counts (received in the month, inside the stream's dates, a counted kind, not a retroactive Title II or SSI payment), on the stated receipts list of any person of the case, whose `for_person` names this person, where that person lives with the dependent (lives_with, CO-1925: (e)(2)(i)). Residence is read only for an award that names the dependent; unstated, cannot determine (decision 4). (e)(2)(ii) is the dependent's own record. A dependent living elsewhere and not paid separately has no part. The irregular part of an irregular award is the dependent's irregular income, so the quarter's first $60 is taken from it.

**Tests.** CO-5511-T26 (the probe and OQ-1348's example: 300) to T34 (the veteran 1,200; living elsewhere 0; home unstated cannot determine; two dependents' records; no for_person; another member's list unstated; the stream ended; the irregular $40 under the $60); CO-5514-T7, T8; CO-6730-T6. Stored CO-850: an aged couple, the wife $400 of Social Security, the veteran $500 and a $900 VA pension with $300 for her: she counts 700, he 1,100, the couple 1,780 > 1,491, none for both (before: 1,480, aged).

## 2. SR-1 (rest): one funeral agreement, and one burial plot, per household member across the holders' lists (fixed, operator P59)

**Text.** 7 CFR 273.8(e)(2) (S2216, with the statute override on CO-5312): "one burial plot per household member, and the value of one funeral agreement per household member"; 10 CCR 2506-1 4.410.D.1, D.4 (S3119, S2967).

**Wrong answer.** snap_countable_funeral_agreements (CO-5312) excluded, on each holder's own list, the largest agreement for each member, so two parents each holding a $1,500 agreement for their child both had theirs excluded (household 0; by the text one agreement, $1,500, counted). CO-5312's precision recorded it. The sibling snap_countable_burial_spaces (CO-5313) had the same reading for plots and the same wrong answer, though unrecorded; fixed with it.

**Why an operator.** For a member, the agreement excluded is the largest any member holds for that member: the greatest, over the holders, of each holder's largest (P58) agreement. A maximum over a group needs three persons at once (the member and two holders compared); `sum`, `count`, `filter` and the binary `max` give none of it for a group of any size. P59 `greatest` (group, value) is `sum`'s sibling: the greatest value over the members, each bound as that person; unknown if the group or any member's value is unknown; 0 for an empty group (as P58 for no record). Drools `accumulate ... max`, Corticon `->max`, fact graph `CollectionMax` (added for P58).

**Fix.** Per member (this person), three new items per kind: the holders in the member's SNAP household (CO-7200, CO-7203); the whole equity of the one excluded, the greatest over holders whose resources the household counts, leaving out an ineligible student and a member receiving SSI or public assistance, whose resources 273.8(e)(17) and 4.411.2.C already exclude (CO-7201, CO-7204); and how many tie for it, counted by the parts the household counts (CO-7202, CO-7205). In CO-5312 and CO-5313 a holder who alone holds one for the member excludes its largest, as before, with no other member's facts read; with two or more holders, a holder of the largest excludes its own part of it divided by the tie count (at least 1). So two parents with equal separate agreements each exclude half of their own (the household excludes the value of one), two owning one agreement jointly each exclude their half whole, and an SSI recipient's larger agreement does not displace another member's.

**Residual corner, stated in CO-7202's precision.** Where every holding tied for the largest whole equity is owned partly by a member whose resources are left out and no fully counted one ties with them (two jointly owned agreements of exactly equal value for the same member, each co-owned with an SSI recipient), the counted parts are added rather than the larger taken. No stored or generated household states it, and telling it apart needs records grouped by `holding_id`, which no operator does. It is not an open question: no reading of the text is in doubt.

**Tests.** CO-5312-T10 (the case: 750 each, 1,500 for the household) to T17; CO-5313-T10 to T14; the new items' tests (separate, joint, SSI, ineligible student, decision 4). Rule tests whose single holder now reads the member's household restate it (CO-5312-T1 to T3, CO-5313-T4, T6, T8, T9, CO-074-T3, T12: relationships stated as their stories give them). Stored CO-851: CO-795's family, the father with $2,000 and a $1,500 agreement for the daughter, the mother another $1,500 for her: 3,500 > 3,000, over_resource_limit, $0 (before: 2,000, eligible, $387).

## 3. CA4-19 / OQ-1254: several transfers in one month, and the first month of the thirty-six (fixed, operator P60)

**Text.** 9 CCR 2503-5 3.520.76 (S2614, S2806; D-80): D.2 "Determine the current Adult Financial Program grant standard. This is the TWFC monthly penalty value." D.3 "Divide the uncompensated value by the TWFC monthly penalty value and round down to the nearest whole number." E "up to a maximum of 36 months depending on the amount of the uncompensated value for each resource transferred." D.6.a, a resource returned in a later month. A.3 "Made within thirty-six (36) months prior to the application date".

**Wrong answer.** Two resources transferred in one month, or transfers on both sides of the application's day in the month thirty-six months before it, were cannot determine (af_twfc_penalty_months, CO-3840).

**Why an operator.** Each record's quotient needs the client's grant standard and each record's placement the client's application date, both facts of the client, inside the sum over the records, which P55 forbids. The standard is one of three parameters, so the divisor alone could be chosen outside the sum; the application's day of the month cannot (only a trick encoding days as month offsets in `that_month` would, which reads wrongly to an approver and is no more an engine-friendly read than an owner's fact). P60 `owner`: inside a record sum, the person whose list the record is on, and that person's fact read as `["of", ["owner"], fact]`; any other person's fact stays a type error there, and a record sum nested in another's condition reads only the owner's list. For engines it is the entity the record's collection belongs to: Drools the joined `Person($p)`, Corticon `receipt.person`, fact graph the record's ancestor (`../../fact`).

**Fix.** CO-3840 sums, over the resources transferred in the month that count and were not returned in whole that month, each one's uncompensated value divided by the client's penalty value (at the determination month) and rounded down, at most 36 each, cut at its own month of return (D.6.a), counting in the month thirty-six months before the application month only those on or after the application's day of the month. The months of a month's resources add; af_is_in_twfc_penalty_period (CO-3841) already runs each month's figure after the last (D.5).

**Tests.** CO-3840-T11 (OQ-1254's example: 2 + 2 = 4, was cannot determine), T14 (October 3 and 20 against October 15: 2, was cannot determine), T15 to T20 (a return in a later month, a return in the same month, the 36-month cap per resource, AND-SO rounding, three transfers across the day, the program unstated). Stored CO-852: CO-131's Lucia giving $1,600 twice in September 2026: 1 + 1 = 2 months, October and November in the period, October's category none (the total would give 3; before, cannot determine).
