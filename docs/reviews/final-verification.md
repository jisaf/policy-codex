## Verdict

**7 P1 found.**

Most of the chapter holds against the current text. The SNAP ten-day, thirty-day non-citizen and thirty-day expense periods, the expedited postponement, the Medical Assistance reasonable opportunity period (notice + 5 + 90 days, with the good-faith extension), the citizenship exemptions of 435.406(a)(1)(iii), the Colorado Works eleven-, twenty- and thirty-day periods, the CCCAP fifteen-day noticing period and LEAP's two weeks plus two all read correctly. Each program's not-applicable gate works, and the three cases I recomputed agree with the engine. The P1s are gaps, where a rule the text states has no item behind it or the outcome leaves it out:

1. LEAP deducts self-employment costs without the receipts 3.755.21.I.2 requires.
2. Colorado Works leaves out the income of a budgetary-unit member outside the assistance unit (a stepparent, a minor parent's parents), though 3.604.3.B.2.e requires its verification.
3. Medicaid and CHP+ statuses read only the person's own elements, so unverified income of a MAGI household member who is not requesting coverage never appears in any status.
4. SNAP never verifies the 40 qualifying quarters (or military connection) that decides a lawful permanent resident's eligibility (4.505.61; 273.2(f)(1)(ii)(A)).
5. SNAP reports `expenses_disallowed`, but the allotment still takes the unverified deductions.
6. The Medicare Savings Program-only branch requires verification of non-liquid resources that 435.952(e) says must be taken on attestation.
7. CCCAP never deducts the verified court-ordered child support that 3.111.K.1 subtracts, so the verification item stands for a deduction no item makes. P1-7 is an income error found through the verification item, and it belongs equally to the child care reviewer.
