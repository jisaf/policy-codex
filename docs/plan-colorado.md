# Colorado build plan

Spec: docs/design-colorado.md. One phase per program family; each phase ends with a policy review, the target household's expectations for that program filled and passing, and a phase note in docs/colorado-notes.md (what worked, what did not, where open questions cluster).

## Phase 1: platform (this branch)
- Volume selector, tag hierarchy with parent groupings, tag and group filters.
- Parameter versions with `to` and per-version source; rule effectivity; case-level relationships with inverses.
- `co` volume skeleton and the target household (CO-01, CO-02).
- Check script and CI run over every volume in the manifest.

## Phase 2: SNAP
Sources: 7 U.S.C. 2011–2036d; 7 CFR 271–273; 10 CCR 2506-1 (Colorado Food Assistance rules); FNS COLA memo for FY2027 and FY2026 (versioning); H.R.1 ABAWD provisions.
Chapters: household composition (purchase-and-prepare, mandatory members, ineligible and disqualified members, minor parents), citizenship and immigrant eligibility, categorical eligibility (BBCE in Colorado), gross and net income tests, deductions (standard, earned income, dependent care, medical for elderly/disabled, child support, shelter and SUA), resources, allotment, work registration and exemptions, ABAWD time limit and exemptions under H.R.1, students, elderly/disabled definitions, homeless shelter deduction, expedited service as an eligibility rule.

## Phase 3: MAGI Medicaid and CHP+
Sources: 42 U.S.C. 1396a, 1397aa–1397mm; 42 CFR 435 subpart B/C/E/G, 457; 10 CCR 2505-10 8.100 (all subsections); Colorado state plan pages; immigrant coverage under Colorado law (Cover All Coloradans).
Chapters: MAGI household construction (tax filer, dependent, non-filer rules, pregnancy counting), income (MAGI-based, 5% disregard), categories (children by age band, pregnant, parents/caretakers, adults, former foster youth, CHP+ children and prenatal), immigrant eligibility, continuous eligibility, presumptive eligibility, retroactive coverage, CHP+ premiums.

## Phase 4: non-MAGI, long-term care, waivers, Buy-In, MSP
Sources: 42 CFR 435 subpart D/F/H/I, 435.700–435.735 (post-eligibility), 435.1005–1012, 1396r-5 (spousal impoverishment), 1396p (transfers, estate recovery as a rule only); 10 CCR 2505-10 8.100.5–8.100.7, 8.110, 8.500s (waivers), 8.900s (PACE); SSI rules 20 CFR 416 by reference.
Chapters: SSI-related income and resource methodology, aged/blind/disabled, institutional eligibility, spousal impoverishment (CSRA, MMMNA, income allocation), transfer penalties and look-back, home equity, post-eligibility patient liability, HCBS waivers (financial rules; level of care supplied), Buy-In (WAwD, CBwD) income and premiums, MSP (QMB/SLMB/QI/QDWI), PACE.

## Phase 5: cash programs
Sources: 45 CFR 260–265; C.R.S. 26-2; 9 CCR 2503-6 (Colorado Works), 9 CCR 2503-5 (Adult Financial), 10 CCR 2505-10 8.940s (OAP health care).
Chapters: Colorado Works assistance unit, income and resources, grant calculation, time limits and exemptions as eligibility rules, work requirements as rules; OAP A/B, AND-SO/CS, AB eligibility and grant.

## Phase 6: CCCAP and LEAP
Sources: 8 CCR 1403-1; 8 CCR 1405-1 (LEAP).
Chapters: eligibility, income limits, activity requirements (as rules).

## Per-phase mechanics
1. Fetch documents into `volumes/co/documents/D-n.md`; append excerpts to sources.md with `Document: D-n`.
2. Author items in batches of ~30 with `rationale`, `nearest` acknowledged, `implemented: engine`, rule tests; run `npm run governance` and `npm run check`.
3. Write program cases (target household first) and fill CO-01/CO-02 expectations for the program.
4. Policy review by a separate reviewer role against the sources: completeness (statute/rule sections not represented), correctness (each rule vs its excerpt), cases (expected outcomes recomputed by hand).
5. Fix round; phase note.

## Completion phases (7–11), started 2026-11-21

Goal: every program a household can apply for through PEAK is in the volume, every element it reads is defined, document collection (verification) is modeled, and every open question that published Colorado or federal rule can answer is answered.

Network as of this date: the Colorado SOS CCR site (sos.state.co.us, official current rule PDFs), eCFR, govinfo, federalregister.gov, medicaid.gov, cms.gov, fns.usda.gov (redirects but serves), Cornell LII and leg.colorado.gov answer; hcpf.colorado.gov, cdhs.colorado.gov, cdec.colorado.gov, colorado.gov and ssa.gov refuse (403). State-published figures are taken from the rule text at SOS, from CMS publications on medicaid.gov (SSI and spousal impoverishment standards, eligibility levels, approved state plan amendments), and from FNS memos.

### Phase 13: interfaces for policy and legal staff (requested 2026-11-21)
After the codex is complete and the stakeholder reviews are fixed. The current app exposes the ledger as items, tables and graphs; policy and legal staff who are not technical need a way in that starts from the questions they ask.
1. Discovery: walkthroughs of the current app by agents playing non-technical policy and legal staff (an HCPF eligibility policy analyst, a CDHS cash-programs policy lead, an assistant attorney general reviewing a rule change, a county eligibility supervisor), each with real tasks: find what a term means, check a figure against the rule, approve a change, answer an open question, explain a denial. Findings ranked by how often they block the task.
2. Design and build 3–5 new views, each a workflow rather than a data dump. Candidates:
   - By aid code: the program/category outcome a household ends in (what CBMS would assign), the rules that lead to it, and the figures it depends on. Requires an aid-code layer mapping each program outcome value to a code. Decision (2026-11-21, product owner): placeholder aid codes for now, clearly marked as placeholders, to be replaced with the CBMS codes when the list is supplied.
   - Open questions: a decision queue by program and owner, each question with the text it turns on, the assumption in force, the items and household outcomes that change if it is answered the other way, and an answer/approve action that opens a change.
   - By role: what HCPF Medicaid, CHP+, CDHS SNAP, cash, CDEC child care and Legal each own, need to approve, and have open.
   - Case walkthrough: step through a household's determination rule by rule, in plain language, with each value, the rule text it rests on, and where an unknown stopped it.
   - Approvals: what is waiting on whom, grouped by change, with the diff in plain language and its effect on outcomes.
3. Usability test of the new views by the same personas; fix round.

### Product-owner decisions (2026-11-21, on the design-limit questions)
- Nothing is deferred "until a case needs it": everything needed for certification at launch is in scope.
- Supplied facts are program-neutral statements of what is true of the person (one immigration status, one income receipt, one relationship). Each program's meaning of a shared word (income, household, caretaker, lawful presence, disability, student, ...) lives in that program's own derived items, never in a second supplied fact.
- Hard-coded figures in derivations become parameters or tables (OQ-82). Facts that another record or edge can supply are read from it, not entered twice (OQ-97, OQ-95). Where a rule asks for a lighter determination (attested rather than computed income, OQ-54), the ledger uses the lighter fact.
- OQ-44 is to be fixed: one immigration-status fact with the full documented category set; per-program derived eligibility. OQ-216, OQ-323, OQ-396 and OQ-413 are to be fixed in full.
Audits (read-only, docs/reviews/audit-*.md): hard-coded numbers; dual entry and derivable facts; heavier-than-needed definitions; same word, different meaning. A fix round follows each.

### Stopping rule (product owner, 2026-11-21)
The Colorado volume is complete when a fresh verification pass (one reviewer per program family, reading the current rule text) finds:
1. no P1: no wrong eligibility or benefit answer, no missing pathway or program, no element a rule reads that is undefined;
2. every remaining open question classed either as a genuine state policy choice (with the text checked recorded) or as blocked on a named document no allowed host serves;
3. if usage allows before the next usage reset, no P2 either (maintainability and approval-risk findings); otherwise every P2 is listed with its disposition.
`npm run check` (with the conformance self-check on every volume), typecheck and the unit tests pass at the commit the pass reviews.

### Phase 13 started early (2026-11-21)
The product owner asked for the UI work to run in parallel with the audit fix tracks. Discovery walkthroughs run now; the aid-code layer waits until the outcome names settle after fix track 1.
Scope (product owner, 2026-11-21): the app defines policy as rules; it is not a rules engine. No intake and no live calculation. The case walkthrough is a read-only step-through of the stored test households, and "what changes" compares stored cases before and after a staged rule change.
Added (product owner, 2026-11-22): a visual design pass after the UI fix round (layout, hierarchy, spacing, a consistent component style, the long walkthrough broken into sections or tabs); and two "blind builder" prototypes, built from the data and the user brief without seeing the current interface, under prototypes/, for comparison.

### Product-owner decision: the engine's role (2026-11-22)
The evaluator in `src/engine/` is the codex's test harness and the producer of the conformance suite, not a product. It runs definitions so rule tests and stored households prove each rule does what its meaning says, explains stored households, and shows a proposed change's effect to approvers. It does not do intake or live calculation for arbitrary households, and it is not to grow into a production engine. Guardrails: every new operator is justified by rule text that needs it and is documented in docs/operators.md (with its P-number in docs/conventions.md) for the engine teams in the same change.

### Product-owner decision: restructure and shared primitives (2026-11-23)
The restructure in docs/design-restructure-proposal.md is approved. Programs are independent: a cross-program read is allowed only for a primitive with one definition everywhere (age, dates, relationships, published figures). Complex program-specific calculations (income, resources, units, homelessness, countable expenses) are never shared; each program builds its own from the shared supplied facts. Every case matters, including edge cases; the stopping rule is unchanged.

### Product-owner decisions: plan for a trustworthy, approvable codex (2026-11-23)
Following docs/reviews/restructure-proposal-review.md and docs/reviews/revised-plan-review.md:
1. **Cross-program links, four classes.** Primitive (one meaning everywhere: age, dates, relationships, published figures); adopted method (a program adopts another method by reference, e.g. SSI-method income for Medicaid), cited; rule-required outcome link (e.g. SNAP categorical eligibility on TANF receipt, Adult Financial barred by a Colorado Works sanction), cited; program calculation (never shared). Undeclared cross-program reads are blocked.
2. **Receipt-record spike now**, time-boxed, on LEAP unearned income, SNAP unearned income and Adult Financial deeming, behind an old-versus-new harness; adopt or reject before any exhaustive review.
3. **Stopping rule tightened:** two independent clean exhaustive passes per program family, plus an external check on a sample (published worked examples or equivalent).
4. **Missing information means "cannot determine" unless the rule says otherwise.** Each supplied fact states once whether it is required or what its rule-given default is; readers do not invent defaults.
Order: (1) keep every probe household, each with an independent hand computation; (2) stale-approval check; (3) receipt spike, decision, then required/default on every fact; (4) change-impact and sibling-copy flags block the merge until dispositioned; (5) no duplicate paths within a program, verification items included; (6) time audit and generated boundary cases; (7) fix open findings; (8) light reviews, two clean exhaustive passes per family, then chapter-by-chapter sign-off by the owning roles.

### Decision, 2026-11-23: adopt receipt records (step 3 outcome)
The product owner adopted receipt records (docs/reviews/receipt-spike.md, Model A) with the spike's four conditions:
1. Record the engine-guardrail exception: a list-of-records input (receipts) and the operators that sum it by kind, attribute and period, justified because the rule texts count money by kind of receipt and by date received; documented in docs/operators.md for engine teams, with the fact-graph reviewer's concerns answered in the same change.
2. Split facts that name two sources (starting with CO-3920) into separate kinds; each program owner signs its counting table row by row, including rows counted only because no rule excludes them.
3. Unearned income first, program by program; earned income and resources are decided explicitly after unearned is done.
4. The old-versus-new harness is the merge gate: every migration commit shows zero unexplained answer changes against every stored household and probe.
Missing receipts: an unstated receipts list is "cannot determine"; an empty list means no unearned income (decision 4 of 2026-11-23).
Step 4 starts from impact-baseline 762a730; the 8 SNAP flags against origin/main (CO-118, CO-138, CO-303, CO-314, CO-335, CO-381) go to the next fix round for a written disposition.

### Receipt records, migration phase 1 (2026-11-23): the transition rule
LEAP, SNAP and Adult Financial (deeming included) read unearned income from receipt records (receipts, CO-5000) through one counting table each (docs/reviews/receipts-migration-1.md); Colorado Works, CCCAP, Medicaid, CHP+ and SUN Bucks read the scalar unearned-income facts until they move. **Transition rule:** until the last reader of the scalar facts moves, a stored household or rule test that states unearned money states it once as receipts and, for the programs not yet migrated, in the old facts, both written from one statement by the translator (`npm run receipts:translate`); `npm run receipts:harness` fails when a stored household's receipts are not the translation of its old facts, and on any answer change it cannot explain. A fact whose last reader moved is retired in the same commit, its stated values kept for the translation only (scripts/receipts/retired-facts.yaml). A household added after the migration may be written in receipts alone. Earned income and resources are decided after unearned income is done (condition 3).

### Receipt records, migration phase 2 (2026-11-23): Colorado Works

The harness baseline for the phase is c29d326 (phase 1 merged). CCCAP, SUN Bucks, Medicaid and CHP+ read the scalar facts until their phases; the transition rule above is unchanged.

### Receipt records, migration phase 2 (2026-11-23): Medicaid and CHP+
Health First Colorado (MAGI and the SSI method: deeming, the spousal allowances, long-term care post-eligibility, the buy-ins, the Medicare Savings Programs) reads unearned income from the receipts through two counting tables, MAGI (ma_magi_receipt_treatment_table, CO-5500; 42 CFR 435.603(e), 10 CCR 2505-10 8.100.4.C) and the SSI method (ma_ssi_receipt_treatment_table, CO-5510; 8.100.5.F with 20 CFR 416 Subpart K as the ledger reads it). CHP+ keeps no table: it reads Medicaid's MAGI household income by the adopted-method link of 10 CCR 2505-3 150.1 (S245), declared on its readers (docs/reviews/receipts-migration-2-medicaid.md). The transition rule above is unchanged, with two differences: the translator also carves Medicaid's VA Aid and Attendance portion into its own receipt, and **no fact is retired in phase 2**: a supplied fact left without a reader stays in the ledger (the translator reads it) until the final cleanup, after the last program moves, retires the orphans together. The harness baseline for phase 2 is c29d326 (phase 1 merged). Colorado Works, CCCAP and SUN Bucks still read the scalar facts.

### Decision, 2026-11-23: earned income and resources move to records too
The product owner chose to extend the one-record-per-item approach to earned income (first) and then resources, after unearned income is complete in every program. Earned income: one record per pay or self-employment receipt (kind, amount, pay date or month, frequency, gross/net and the earmarks such as PASS set-asides or in-kind pay), with each program's counting table deciding what counts and which disregards apply. Resources: one record per holding (kind, owner(s), value, valuation date, and transfer/encumbrance details), with each program's countable/excluded table. Same gate as unearned income: the old-versus-new harness shows zero unexplained answer changes; every allowed difference is a cited fix or a product-owner decision.

### Earned-income records, E0 (2026-11-23): the foundation
Step E0 of docs/design-earned-and-resource-records.md 4.2, by the integration owner, under decisions D1-D11 (design section 7; docs/reviews/earnings-e0.md). The `earnings` list (CO-5100) is in the ledger beside `receipts`, with 22 kinds (each a source of pay or a kind of business cost) and its fields, each required, defaulted by its rule (D3) or saying what absence means. Seven counting tables (snap_, leap_, af_, cw_, cccap_, ma_magi_ and ma_ssi_earnings_treatment_table, CO-5101 to CO-5107; 154 rows) give every kind today's reading, each row cited or marked for the owner's signature (76 rows, five of them findings where today's reading differs from the text on file: OQ-1021 to OQ-1023). CHP+ keeps no table (it adopts MAGI, 10 CCR 2505-3 150.1); SUN Bucks reads none. The boundary with receipts (design 2.3, OQ-1020) is stated in every receipt table: special pay and payments for a room or meals only are earnings, kept in receipts with their rows unchanged until each program reads its earnings table, and retired from receipts in E2. **No rule reads `earnings` in E0, and E0 changes no answer.** **Transition rule (as for receipts):** until a program's E1 track moves its readers, a stored household or rule test that states earned income states it in the scalar earned facts and as earnings records, both written by the translator from one statement (`npm run records:translate`); `npm run records:harness -- --list earnings` fails when a household's or test's records are not the translation, on a boundary finding, and on any answer change no allowed-differences file explains. The thirty-day wages and the job-began flag (CO-3910, CO-3923) are not restated (a thirty-day total is not one pay); the LEAP and CCCAP tracks state those pays with their dates. A household added after E0 may be written in earnings alone once its programs read them. The harness registry is split per track (scripts/records/programs/<track>.ts and scripts/records/allowed/e1-<track>.yaml), so the six E1 tracks (SNAP, LEAP, Adult Financial, Colorado Works, CCCAP, Medicaid with CHP+) run in parallel without editing a shared list; they merge one at a time in the order LEAP, Colorado Works, Adult Financial, CCCAP, SNAP, Medicaid (design 4.3, rule 5). Each track's harness baseline is the end of E0 (EARNINGS_BASELINE, scripts/records/harness.ts).

### E1 CCCAP and LEAP (2026-11-23)
Earned-income records, step E1, for child care, energy assistance and SUN Bucks (docs/reviews/records-earnings-care-energy.md). CCCAP and LEAP read the earnings records (earnings, CO-5100) through their own counting tables (CO-5105, CO-5102) and no scalar earned-income fact; SUN Bucks reads no earnings. CCCAP reads the pays dated in the thirty days before the application or redetermination date, a new case fact (cccap_application_or_redetermination_date, CO-5150), and an undated pay as the month's (8 CCR 1403-1 3.111.H.4-5, OQ-680). Fixes the records expose, each a cited class of the harness: LEAP counts a boarder's payment for room and board as it counted one for a room or meals only (3.752.21); CCCAP's J.28 pools irregular earnings with irregular receipts; a PASS set-aside is taken off the pay it is part of. Decision 4 applied to the readers' last defaults over receipts and earnings in these programs (CCCAP's in-kind, vendor and boarder pay, the launch-period hours, SUN Bucks' TANF receipts of a child). The CCCAP T-9 boundary rows are settled by 45 CFR 98.20(a)(1) and 3.103.YY; Child Welfare Child Care's thirteen-year-old is OQ-1163. Duplicate paths D-L2 and D-K2 are merged; D-K1, D-L1, D-K3, D-K4 and D-K5 stay listed with the reason. The receipts harness's generated households state no earnings, so its CCCAP and LEAP comparisons of them are cannot determine from E1 (class E1-G; request R-4); the records harness compares those readings.

### E1 SNAP (2026-11-23): SNAP reads the earnings records
SNAP's earned-income readers read the earnings records (CO-5100) through snap_earnings_treatment_table (CO-5101) (docs/reviews/records-earnings-snap.md): each pay is converted by its own frequency under 10 CCR 2506-1 4.402.A.2 (snap_regular_wages_conversion_factor, CO-4682, retired), the counted pay is summed record by record (CO-5113), the business, the farm and boarders are read from their own records, and no SNAP earned-income reader keeps a default for a fact not stated (decision 4): a person who states no earnings is cannot determine, and a business record must say whether it is the household's annual support (OQ-1101). Wages an employer diverts to a third party for a household expense are counted (4.405.H.4; OQ-1021 resolved). Special pay is read as earnings (SNAP's receipt row read_from_earnings); a payment for meals only or a room only stays read from the receipts while OQ-996 is open, and E2 must carry it when those receipt kinds leave. The prorated initial month is capped for disqualified members as the full month is (duplicate-paths D-S3), unpaid work hours are verified as they are counted (D-S6), and one quarter test serves earned and unearned irregular income (D-S4). The transition rule of E0 is unchanged for the other programs. Open for the integration owner: the receipts harness's generated households state no earnings records, so the receipts gate cannot pass for a program that reads them until the harness translates their earnings (review, section 9).
